Title: Classic French Onion Soup (Soupe à l'Oignon Gratinée)
Cuisine: French
Dietary Tags: Vegetarian Option (use vegetable broth), Contains Gluten, Contains Dairy
Prep Time: 20 minutes
Cook Time: 1 hour 20 minutes
Servings: 4

Ingredients:
- 1.5 kg (3.3 lbs) yellow onions (about 6 large), halved and very thinly sliced
- 4 tbsp unsalted butter
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 1 tsp granulated sugar
- 1 tsp salt
- 4 garlic cloves, minced
- 1/2 cup (120ml) dry white wine (Chardonnay or Sauvignon Blanc) or dry sherry
- 6 cups (1.4 liters) high-quality beef broth (or vegetable broth for vegetarian)
- 1 tbsp Worcestershire sauce
- 2 sprigs fresh thyme (or 1/2 tsp dried thyme)
- 2 bay leaves
- Salt and freshly cracked black pepper to taste

Gratin Topping:
- 8 thick slices (about 1.5 cm / 3/4 inch) baguette, cut on a slight diagonal
- 2 cups (200g) Gruyère cheese, freshly grated (do NOT use pre-shredded)
- 1/2 cup (50g) Comté or Emmental cheese, grated (optional, for additional depth)

Instructions:
1. Melt butter with olive oil in a large heavy-bottomed Dutch oven or pot over medium heat.
2. Add all the sliced onions, sugar, and 1 tsp salt. Stir to coat. Cover and cook for 10 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the onions have softened and released their liquid.
3. Uncover, reduce heat to medium-low, and continue cooking, stirring every 5–8 minutes, for 50–60 minutes. This is the most important step: do not rush it. The onions will slowly turn golden, then amber, then a deep rich brown as their natural sugars caramelize. If they begin to stick, add 1–2 tbsp of broth and scrape the bottom.
4. The onions are ready when they are a deep mahogany brown with a jammy, melt-in-your-mouth consistency and the bottom of the pot has a rich fond (browned layer) — this develops the soup's full depth of flavor.
5. Add garlic and cook for 2 minutes. Pour in the white wine and stir vigorously, scraping all the fond from the bottom. Cook until the wine evaporates, about 3 minutes.
6. Add the beef broth, Worcestershire sauce, thyme, and bay leaves. Bring to a boil, then reduce and simmer for 20 minutes. Remove thyme sprigs and bay leaves. Season with salt and pepper.
7. Meanwhile, preheat oven to 375°F (190°C). Arrange baguette slices on a baking sheet and toast for 8–10 minutes, turning once, until dry and golden. Set aside.
8. Turn oven to broil.
9. Ladle the hot soup into 4 oven-safe French onion soup crocks or ramekins, filling to about 1 inch below the rim.
10. Float 2 toasted baguette slices on the surface of each bowl.
11. Pile the grated Gruyère (and Comté if using) generously over the bread — it should cover the bread and slightly overlap the edges of the bowl for that signature melted-and-charred crust.
12. Place the crocks on a baking sheet and broil for 3–5 minutes, watching closely, until the cheese is bubbling, golden, and has charred edges in spots.
13. Use caution — the crocks will be extremely hot. Serve immediately.
