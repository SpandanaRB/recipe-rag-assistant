Title: Authentic Spaghetti Carbonara
Cuisine: Italian
Dietary Tags: Contains Gluten, Contains Dairy, High-Protein
Prep Time: 10 minutes
Cook Time: 20 minutes
Servings: 4

Ingredients:
- 400g (14 oz) spaghetti or rigatoni
- 200g (7 oz) guanciale (cured pork cheek) or high-quality pancetta, cut into small cubes or strips
- 4 large egg yolks + 1 whole egg
- 100g (1 cup) Pecorino Romano cheese, finely grated
- 50g (1/2 cup) Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
- 2 garlic cloves, lightly crushed (optional, traditional recipes omit)
- Freshly cracked black pepper, generously applied
- Salt, for pasta water (do not salt the sauce — the cheeses and guanciale are very salty)
- Reserved pasta cooking water (about 1 cup)

Instructions:
1. Bring a large pot of generously salted water to a rolling boil. Add spaghetti and cook until just al dente (1 minute less than package directions). Reserve at least 1 cup of starchy pasta water before draining.
2. While pasta cooks, place guanciale in a cold, large skillet. Turn heat to medium and cook, stirring occasionally, for 8–10 minutes until the fat renders and the meat is golden and crisp but not hard. Remove from heat.
3. If using garlic, add it to the rendered fat for 1–2 minutes, then discard before adding the pasta.
4. In a bowl, vigorously whisk together egg yolks, the whole egg, and the grated Pecorino Romano and Parmigiano-Reggiano until a thick, smooth paste forms. Season generously with cracked black pepper.
5. Add 2–3 tablespoons of the hot pasta water to the egg and cheese mixture and whisk quickly to temper it (this prevents scrambling).
6. Drain the cooked spaghetti and immediately add it to the skillet with the guanciale (off the heat or on very low heat). Toss vigorously to coat the pasta in the rendered fat.
7. Remove the pan completely from heat. Add the egg-cheese mixture and toss rapidly with tongs or two forks, adding reserved pasta water a tablespoon at a time, until the sauce is silky, glossy, and clings to every strand. The residual heat cooks the eggs; keep everything moving so the eggs do not scramble.
8. The finished sauce should coat the pasta like a creamy, emulsified sauce with no visible pooling.
9. Divide immediately into warmed serving bowls.
10. Top with extra grated Pecorino, more cracked black pepper, and extra guanciale. Serve at once — carbonara does not wait.
