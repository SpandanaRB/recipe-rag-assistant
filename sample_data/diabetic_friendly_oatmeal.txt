Title: Diabetic-Friendly Cinnamon Steel-Cut Oatmeal with Berries
Cuisine: American
Dietary Tags: Diabetic-Friendly, Low Glycemic Index, Vegan, Gluten-Free (certified GF oats), High-Fiber, Dairy-Free
Prep Time: 5 minutes
Cook Time: 30 minutes
Servings: 2

Ingredients:
- 1 cup (180g) certified gluten-free steel-cut oats (NOT instant — steel-cut oats have a much lower glycemic index)
- 2 cups (480ml) water
- 1 cup (240ml) unsweetened almond milk (or any low-calorie plant milk)
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon
- 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
- 1/4 tsp pure vanilla extract
- 1–2 tsp erythritol, stevia, or monk fruit sweetener (to taste; these do not spike blood sugar)
- Pinch of salt

Toppings:
- 1/2 cup (75g) fresh blueberries (low GI fruit, high in antioxidants)
- 1/2 cup (75g) fresh strawberries, sliced
- 2 tbsp ground flaxseed (adds healthy omega-3s and soluble fiber)
- 2 tbsp unsalted walnuts, roughly chopped (healthy fats slow glucose absorption)
- 1 tsp chia seeds
- Dash of cinnamon on top

Instructions:
1. Combine steel-cut oats, water, and almond milk in a medium saucepan over medium-high heat.
2. Bring to a gentle boil, then immediately reduce heat to low. Stir well and add the cinnamon, nutmeg, vanilla, and a pinch of salt.
3. Simmer uncovered, stirring every 5 minutes, for 25–30 minutes until the oats are creamy and tender but still have a slight chew. Steel-cut oats should retain some texture — this helps maintain a low glycemic response.
4. Remove from heat. Stir in the erythritol or preferred no-calorie sweetener to taste.
5. Divide between two bowls.
6. Top each bowl with half the blueberries and strawberries.
7. Sprinkle ground flaxseed and chia seeds over the fruit.
8. Add chopped walnuts for healthy fat and crunch.
9. Finish with an extra dash of cinnamon on top.
10. Serve immediately. For meal prep, store cooled oatmeal in the refrigerator for up to 4 days — reheat with a splash of almond milk, adding toppings fresh each morning.

Diabetic-Friendly Tips:
- Steel-cut oats have a glycemic index (GI) of about 42, compared to 66 for rolled oats and 83 for instant oats.
- Cinnamon has been shown in studies to improve insulin sensitivity; use generously.
- Pairing oats with fat (walnuts) and fiber (flax, chia) further slows glucose absorption.
- Avoid dried fruit, honey, maple syrup, and brown sugar — these spike blood sugar significantly.
- Check your blood glucose 2 hours after eating to understand your personal response.
